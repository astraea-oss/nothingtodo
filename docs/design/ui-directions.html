NothingToDo · Interface directions · v0.2 · 28 Oct 2026

Nothing
To.Do

Four directions for a monochrome, dot-matrix task app that treats your upcoming reminders like rows in a well-kept table. Same data, same rules, four ways to read it.

01Ledger

A spreadsheet with a formula bar for capture.

02Strip

A week strip on top of compact time rows.

03Gutter

Ledger rows on a time rail. Tap the rail to insert.

04Index

One long agenda with a dot scrubber for the next 30 days.

Shared language · applies to all four

The rules

T flips light / dark · P switches tab
← → to move

Colour: three and a warning

#000
#1A1A1A
#F4F4F2
#D71921
  • Greyscale carries the structure. Hierarchy comes from weight, size and position, never from hue.
  • Red means only one of three things: now, overdue, or conflict. If everything is red, nothing is.
  • No shadows, no gradients on content. Hairlines and dotted rules separate the data.
  • Big round touch targets (pills, circles) sit on top of square, gridded data.

Type: three voices

16:00Dot · numbers you glance at
REMIND_AT ↑Mono · labels, cells
Pick up prescriptionGrotesk · your words

Large dot type uses a hand-drawn 9-row matrix face: single-dot strokes, corners rounded by leaving a dot out, caps and digits only. Every size shares the same grid. Small labels can use a dot font (stand-in Doto; NDot if licensable). Dot type is only for counts, times and headings. Your task titles always use a plain, readable face.

Row states from the real data model

Open: completed = false Done: dimmed and struck, stays in place until the day rolls over Overdue: remind_at is in the past and the task isn't done Pending: a local edit that hasn't synced yet (dirty) Conflict: the remote changed while you were offline. You choose which version wins. —Synced: no glyph at all. A synced row should look calm. ✕Tombstone: deleted rows leave the list but stay recoverable in a "Deleted" view until the delete syncs

Ledger

Your week as one long spreadsheet. Day bands act as group headers, a red NOW line cuts through, and you add a task by typing a formula: "Call bank fri 9am".

Columns
# · ✓ · Title · Due · In (countdown) · Sync

A formula bar pinned near the thumb. It parses dates as you type and shows the result underneath before you commit.
Edit
Tap a row to expand it, tap a cell to edit just that value. Swipe right to complete, swipe left to snooze.
Best for
Scanning 20+ upcoming items fast. Closest to "a beautiful Excel".
Test for
Is a natural-language date parser trustworthy enough? Is it too dense at arm's length?

Direction · week strip × compact rows
02

Strip

A compact date header and a week strip with dot loads sit on top of Gutter's compact rows. The strip picks the day and the list stays a dense table, so you get a calendar's navigation with a spreadsheet's density.

Navigate
Tap a day in the strip or swipe the list sideways to change day. Pulling the strip down could grow it into a full month with the same dot loads.
Capture
Type into the input bar and the view follows what you write. "fri 8am" jumps the strip to Friday and shows the new row in place, marked red, before you press Add.
Reschedule
Press and hold a row, then drop it on a day in the strip. It keeps its time and moves to the new day.
Best for
Day-at-a-time use while the rest of the week stays one tap away.
Test for
Is a 7-day strip enough, or does it need to scroll into next week? Is drag-to-strip discoverable?

03

Press and hold a row, then drag it along the rail to a new time.
Best for
Ledger density with the order of the day made visible.

Direction · a longer horizon
04

Index

One continuous agenda covering the next 30 days. A column of dots on the right edge works like a phone's A–Z index, except each dot is a day and its size shows how busy that day is.

Scroll
Day headers stick to the top as you scroll. Days with nothing on are skipped.
Scrub
Drag the dot index with your thumb and a bubble shows the date plus a preview of that day's rows. Let go to jump there.
Index dots
Size = number of tasks, red = overdue, ring = today. Month labels sit between the dots.
Best for
"What's coming on Friday?" and "When's the passport thing?" without leaving the list.
Test for
The index sits right where an edge-swipe "back" gesture starts. Needs testing on Android gesture navigation.

Part 2 · entry types · built on 02 Strip with A · Shapes

Types

Three priorities
three sound behaviours

Alarm

High priority
Sound
Rings until you stop it. Stopping takes a press-and-hold.
Time
Required, to the minute. Can repeat.
Snooze
One tap, 5 or 10 minutes.
Examples
Take antibiotics · wake up · leave for the airport
In lists
Filled double ring. The loudest thing on screen, but not red.

Reminder

Medium priority
Sound
One chime at the time, then it waits as a notification.
Time
Required. This is today's app behaviour.
Snooze
From the notification.
Examples
Pick up prescription · call Mum · push the release tag
In lists
Hollow ring, the node used everywhere in part 1.

Marker · NAME TBD

Varies · no set time
Sound
None. Optionally listed in a quiet morning summary a few days ahead.
Time
No time. A day, or a span of days.
Examples
Sam's birthday · Halloween · council tax due
In lists
Small square, listed as an all-day row.
Name ideas
Marker · Date · Occasion · Milestone · Note
Data modelA task needs a kind (alarm, reminder or marker), and markers store a day instead of a time. That's a schema v2 migration in core, with existing tasks becoming reminders.
Alarms on AndroidRinging until stopped while the app is closed needs native alarm scheduling and a full-screen alert, tested on a device. Until then alarms only ring while the app is running, and the UI should say so.
Red stays reservedAlarms get the heaviest weight (filled, inverted) but not red. Red still means now, overdue and conflict, plus an alarm that is ringing right now.
Entry types · one list, shape = type
A

Shapes

One list in time order, where the node on the rail tells you the type: a filled double ring for an alarm, a hollow ring for a reminder, a square for a marker. Markers sit at the top of their day as all-day rows.

In the list
Alarms add a small RINGS tag. Markers have no time, just "ALL DAY".
Capture
The add sheet opens with a three-way switch. The fields under it change: alarms get sound, snooze and repeat, reminders get just a time, markers drop the time completely.
Best for
The smallest change to 02 Strip. Type is readable at a glance without adding sections.
Test for
Are the three shapes distinct at arm's length? Does a silent marker at the top of the day get noticed?

Adding · type it, then fix the chips · set aside
A

One line

Everything can be typed in one line. Each part the app understands turns into a chip under the input. Tap a chip to change it, and fields you haven't set wait as dashed chips. No form unless you ask for one.

Typing
"fri 8am", "every 8h", "yearly", "for 45m", "warn 1w", "!!" for an alarm. Anything it doesn't understand stays in the title.
Chips
Type · When · Repeat · Warn. Tapping one opens a small picker in place of the keyboard, with the repeat presets as a grid.
Unset
Dashed chips (+ time, + notes) suggest what's missing without asking.
Best for
Speed. A full birthday in one sentence.
Test for
Parsing has to be trustworthy. Does every chip need to be visible before Add, so nothing is guessed silently?

More

The sheet asks for three things: title, type and when. Everything else folds into one "More" row that lists what's inside. Open it and every field is a row with its current value, like a record in a table.

B1

Tiles

Same structure as B: a short sheet, then pull up for the rest. Here the details become a grid of widget tiles like the Nothing home screen, each with a big dot numeral, so it reads like a set of readouts rather than a settings list.

Short sheet
Three round type keys instead of a text switch. The time sits in a dark readout panel. A dot grip and the tops of the detail tiles peeking at the bottom invite the pull up.
Tiles
Type · When · Duration · Repeat (a ring of 12 months, lit on the months it repeats) · Warn · Sound (a dot waveform of the chime) · Snooze · Notes. Tiles follow the type: markers drop Sound and Snooze, alarms ring until stopped. Tap a tile to edit it in place.
Signature
The red dot appears once, on the Add button.
Best for
Seeing the whole entry at a glance, and it looks like it belongs next to Nothing widgets.
Test for
Do tiles take more room than rows? Is "6" plus a month ring readable as "every 6 months"?

Adding · B1 tiles, opened · chosen
B1

Tile edit

Every field has a tile. Tapping one opens it in place to full width with its presets, and the other tiles dim. Nothing else moves, so you never leave the sheet.
